NDI vs Dante

NDI and Dante are both important technologies in connected media environments, but they serve different purposes and should be understood in the context of the wider system.

What NDI is used for

NDI is commonly associated with moving video and related media signals over IP networks. It is often used in production and presentation environments where flexible transport and streamlined connectivity are valuable.

What Dante is used for

Dante is strongly associated with audio networking and is widely used where dependable transport of audio across connected environments is a priority. It can play a central role in professional AV and audio-focused systems.

Why they are often discussed together

Organisations often encounter both technologies as they modernise workflows and move away from isolated systems toward more connected infrastructure. In practice, the real question is often not which one is better in isolation, but how each fits into the wider technical environment.

What matters most in planning

The important decisions usually sit around workflow design, network capacity, interoperability, device compatibility, and operational needs. Understanding where each technology fits helps avoid over-simplified decisions and supports better long-term design.
